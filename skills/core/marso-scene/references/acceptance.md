# Scene acceptance and handoffs

## Define the complete result before creating assets

Keep the requested deliverable and review permissions in the existing run record. When a user continues from blockout to a finished scene, carry unfinished groups forward and update the completion target. Preserve accepted geometry and service results. A pilot tests the route; it does not reduce the requested population or appearance variety.

For reference-led work, inspect the actual anchor image and account for the regions that establish its identity: architecture/openings, major furniture, dense assortments, visible fixtures, supporting hardware, contents and visible exterior context. Record only meaningful groups, not a separate task for every screw. Treat detection results and the asset plan as provisional coverage, even when their schemas validate. A feature absent from the plan is not automatically outside the user's scope. Explicit item selections or exclusions do constrain scope; do not expand those into a full reconstruction.

For each defining group, retain compact fields in the existing record:

| Field | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Source evidence | Image/view and region, or explicit design requirement; separate observation from inference. |
| Master / appearance / population | Distinct shapes, required style sets, approximate counts or ranges, relative sizes and grouping. |
| Route and owner | Reuse, generate, model or kit; include contents inside compound groups. |
| Evidence and next action | Latest inspected view and source revision; unresolved geometry, material or placement issue and its next action. |

For example, a cabinet with a varied display needs the cabinet, distinct contents and their populated arrangement. One good container and three colours do not establish coverage of differently shaped contents. Exact counts at cropped edges and illegible detail can be design assumptions; missing prominent silhouettes cannot.

## Use three decision points, not a check for every instance

1. **Layout:** inspect a plan and a reference-matched eye view. Match openings, dominant masses, relative screen coverage and major groups before expensive detail. Establish the comparison camera before moving assets to compensate for perspective. A presentation camera may differ, but cannot replace this comparison. Review normally continues without asking the user unless scope or ambiguity requires it.
2. **Masters and finishing pilot:** inspect each distinct master at its closest intended view, then test a representative appearance before expanding dependent instances/styles. Use already accepted sources when suitable. Small props inside a large group still need a deliberate creation route; don't promote crude placeholders because they are small in the room. Model approval before Marso finishing remains separate and follows the user's existing waiver/approval. A passed pilot advances to the rest of the population, not to delivery.
3. **Populated scene:** compare the whole source with the actual material-shaded scene, plus a close view only where density or defects require it. Check shape-family coverage, relative size, occupied area, clustering/orientation, supports and material character. Objects outside the camera or hidden by a changed pose do not prove visible coverage. Counts, successful jobs and packed files support technical validation, not visual acceptance.

Do not combine all three into a final render after modelling and cloud work are done. Reuse earlier evidence for unchanged masters. After a local correction, inspect the affected view and recheck whole-scene coverage once; don't repeat full atlas audits or every master render. Retain progress while native jobs wait. Correct the largest visible mismatch first, rather than polishing tiny details in an incomplete scene.

## Own the handoff through delivery

Carry the source image, scope, accepted camera/shell, master and style identities, outstanding population, review permissions and unresolved actions through stage handoffs. Partial completion by a specialist is input to the next stage, not permission for the coordinating agent to stop. Record actual inspected artifacts; a note saying "looks good" without opening the image is not a review.

Review from the user's brief and source image before reading the work list's completion claims. When an independent reviewer is available and authorized, give it those raw inputs and actual previews, not a desired verdict. Its role is to identify visible departures; it cannot infer topology, packed maps or successful service execution from a render. Independent review is useful for complex scenes, not a required new agent for every build.

Missing defining groups, wrong proportions/contacts, unfinished masters and visible material failures require correction before calling a requested reconstruction complete. Follow user-approved simplification; tolerate minor distant detail or uncertainty that does not change the intended result. If an iteration limit or unavailable capability prevents repair, save and report a partial result with the specific blocker; do not relabel it complete or ask the user to rediscover the gap.

A requested final render starts after the inexpensive populated-scene review passes. Check the actual final output too: newly visible undersides, glass reflections and light emitters can reveal faults absent from an earlier view. Preserve originals, use the texture workflow for material diagnosis and keep local shader changes distinguishable from returned Marso maps. Do not hide defects with normal-map edits, grading or a flattering camera. Deliver the requested output and remaining material limitations honestly; a limitations list is not authorization to omit requested work.
