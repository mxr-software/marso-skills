# Retexture UV acceptance

Retexture preparation must provide one verified 0-1 layout for each texture target, with unique non-mirrored coverage for all textured surfaces. Authoring extra UV layers is fine; identify the exact layer used by export and materials. Apply these requirements to the upload geometry, including evaluated modifiers and repeated parts, not just the unmodified source cage.

## Choose an unwrap

Choose seams and unwrap methods per surface family. Keep broad surfaces together where distortion permits; cut at construction breaks, less visible returns and changes of curvature. A tube may need a longitudinal seam and separate caps; a shell may need several panels. Inspect stretching on bends: mapping a whole elbow by centreline length does not equalise density on its inner and outer radii.

Use seam-based Angle Based or Minimum Stretch unwrapping when supported, comparing the checker and layout on the working target. Automatic projection remains suitable for simple planar parts when it produces useful islands; do not Smart UV project a whole mixed asset merely because the command succeeds. Repair local seam problems rather than repeatedly replacing every island. Grain-direction alignment is optional when the brief needs it, not a prerequisite for a usable Retexture layout.

Judge distortion, island fragmentation, relative texel density and final pixel spacing together. Packing cannot repair a stretched island. Prefer fewer useful charts over many tiny slivers, but do not preserve one large island at the cost of severe distortion. Normalise relative density before shared packing; allow deliberate emphasis on close-view surfaces. Measure spacing at the intended texture resolution after packing and any subsequent rescaling. Use concave packing when its gain is useful, without treating maximum fill as the sole objective.

Metric coordinates can establish relative scale before packing. Establish broad-surface density, choose deliberate small-detail exceptions, then pack with a common scale factor that preserves those choices, positive orientation and resolution-appropriate padding. Do not impose one island aspect ratio, segment length or fill percentage on every object.

When long strips or hollow rings dominate the atlas bounds or leave large unusable gaps, compare an alternate layout on the same geometry: cut the ring at a less visible seam and unwrap its band, or divide a long perimeter at a construction break. An annular surface need not stay an annular UV chart. Check distortion before accepting the change; do not force a curved or tapered surface into a rectangle merely to increase fill. Try fitting compatible charts into ring interiors where that genuinely works. Keep the version that improves useful sampling and packing without unacceptable seams or stretch. Enlarging a wasteful ring can magnify the waste; address its chart shape before repeatedly increasing its scale.

Preserve a sound direct mapping when it already fits a simple surface. For example, a straight rounded member can use cumulative perimeter distance for its side chart and planar end charts. Equal vertex spacing in UV space distorts a perimeter whose mesh samples are unevenly spaced. Relaxing a long, thin chart can also make it worse: solver options that prevent local flips do not certify global non-overlap. Compare before and after, retain the better valid layout, and run the complete audit after packing.

If concave packing with arbitrary rotation stalls or crashes, save the authored seams/UVs and retry only packing on a working copy with cardinal rotation and, if needed, bounding-box packing. Treat that result as a fallback to inspect, not automatic final acceptance. Keep the last valid layout before a bounded comparison; do not rerun modelling, baking or generation to resolve a local packing problem.

For curated UVs, use a separate single-mesh working target if the current assembly preparation would replace the layout. Preserve masters, connector frames and material-role information. Bake a colour-only reference without illumination; restore any temporarily changed metallic/transmission settings and links even on failure. The add-on's existing assembly bake already handles its own temporary shader changes.

## Exclude only permanently hidden surfaces

Record an exclusion reason and the object's allowed states. Internal faces of a sealed, fixed construction may be excluded from a texture working copy or use the add-on's collapsed-UV treatment. A cabinet interior, a door's back and a kit attachment end may become visible and normally need valid UV area.

Do not infer permanent invisibility from a single camera, hidden-in-viewport flags or one assembled pose. Inspect candidate hidden faces against all intended states. Marso's current visibility sampling is evidence for the current arrangement, not a proof for every possible arrangement. Preserve uncertain faces. Never delete required functional/collision geometry just to improve texture coverage.

## Audit

The optional [audit helper](../scripts/audit_uv.py) runs inside Blender. Call `audit_uv(objects, uv_name=..., excluded_faces=...)` on the actual mesh objects comprising one texture target. `excluded_faces` maps object names to polygon indices verified permanently hidden; omission excludes nothing. The helper does not determine visibility or modify data. Run on independent evaluated copies if modifiers change the upload geometry.

It reports negative UV winding (a mirrored-orientation diagnostic), zero-area triangles, out-of-tile coordinates, positive-area triangle overlaps and density. Touching island boundaries alone are not counted as overlaps. Geometry normals and negative object scale must also be checked: the winding diagnostic does not establish correct mesh orientation. Excluded faces are omitted from the area/overlap audit and counted separately, not silently accepted as textured faces.

Fix reported failures before calling the layout ready. Inspect seams, minimum island spacing, distortion and a checker in multiple views separately; the helper does not certify those. In particular, a clean overlap result does not prove sufficient padding.

The optional [quality measurement helper](../scripts/measure_uv_quality.py) complements that audit. Call `measure_uv_quality(objects, uv_name=..., resolution=4096, padding_px=..., excluded_faces=...)` on the same evaluated target. It measures connected chart count, area-weighted density and directional stretch, tile-border spacing and whether separate chart boundaries meet the requested pixel spacing. Its per-chart area, bounds and `area_perimeter_width_proxy_px` help locate undersampled candidates; neither that proxy nor the bounding box establishes a minimum local width. Choose padding for the actual resolution/filtering; do not treat a script default as a service requirement. `padding_passed` is only a spacing check, not overall UV acceptance. The helper does not measure proximity within a single chart or determine hidden faces.

Anisotropy of 1 means equal local scale in both directions; larger values reveal directional stretch that area-based density can miss. Compare the distribution and checker rather than imposing one universal threshold. UV area sum represents occupancy only after the overlap and bounds audit passes. Island count alone is not a quality score. Recheck after all packing, atlas rescaling and transfer operations.

Inspect the smallest charts as well as area-weighted statistics. An otherwise clean layout can contain bevel fragments with only a few pixels of area. For a simple rounded member, a continuous side chart and deliberate end charts may preserve detail better than projecting each bevel patch separately. Repair the seams or unwrap locally, then compare distortion and padding again; do not enlarge every tiny face indiscriminately.

Check useful chart width at the lowest intended texture resolution, not just chart area or the largest atlas size. Infer a sensible lower resolution for the delivery/use and state it; do not impose 1K on every asset or interview the user about routine settings. A long strip can have substantial area yet be only one pixel thick. After resolving avoidable fragmentation and wasteful chart shapes, deliberately increase allocation for small visible details that still need their own charts. Do not subsequently normalise away that allocation. As a practical starting point, aim for several pixels across important narrow strips at the chosen lower resolution, then inspect an actual checker or downsampled colour guide at that resolution. This is a sampling target, not a guarantee for every mip level.

Measure representative cross-sections across the strip, including narrow bends and returns, rather than using its bounding box alone. For a tapered chart, distinguish the useful surface from a mathematical tip. Area/perimeter estimates can identify candidates but can conceal local necks. Record what was measured, recheck gaps and tile borders after the final pack, and disclose unresolved undersampling. A resolution argument to a measurement helper is not evidence that an image at that resolution was visually inspected.

After UV changes, re-bake the working colour guide and re-export; old images no longer correspond to the new atlas. Reopen the prepared file and inspect the actual exported UV layer, colour texture binding and object inventory. Compare geometry to the preserved source with coordinates and face/loop correspondence, accounting for documented exclusions or export splitting; counts alone are insufficient. Keep model quality, UV validity, sampling/packing and cloud-result acceptance separate in the handoff.

## Exported vertex splits

An exporter can split a vertex at a hard normal or other attribute boundary without splitting the UV surface. The default quality helper uses mesh vertex identity and UVs for chart connectivity, so an imported GLB can report extra charts or zero padding where its source layout was continuous. Preserve the raw result and check the source/export correspondence before diagnosing a seam defect.

For that case only, compare `measure_uv_quality(..., match_split_vertices=True)` on the imported copy. This read-only diagnostic joins chart connectivity across exactly matching local positions and UV endpoints with opposite directed edges inside one object. It does not weld or modify geometry, bridge approximate matches, or establish that separate coincident parts should be joined. Ambiguous matches stay separate and are reported. Retain both reports and run the overlap/winding audit independently; positional matching must never conceal stacked faces, real seams or changed exported UVs. If the diagnostic cannot establish the correspondence, report the unresolved discrepancy instead of modifying the deliverable merely to make a metric pass.

For non-overlapping in-tile UVs with reasonably uniform density, estimated pixels/metre is `resolution * sqrt(UV_area / surface_area_m2)`. Report density as an estimate, not proof of uniformity. Use per-triangle minimum/maximum density to find severe stretching; investigate tiny triangles separately. Equal atlas resolution and fill across different surface areas do not mean equal density.

Keep test evidence separated: local UV preparation and add-on preflight can pass without proving that a cloud Retexture job will return good maps. Verify an actual generated result before claiming end-to-end success.
